The Formula Sheet

Every load-bearing formula in the book, chapter by chapter, each with a plain-English reading and a link to where it is taught.

01Arithmetic

$$\gcd(a,b)\cdot \operatorname{lcm}(a,b) = a\,b$$

Read it as the greatest common divisor and least common multiple split the prime factors of $ab$ between them, without loss or overlap. § GCD

$$\frac{a}{b}+\frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad+bc}{bd}$$

Read it as to add fractions, first re-express both on a common denominator, then add numerators. § Fractions

$$x\% \text{ of } y \;=\; \frac{x}{100}\, y$$

Read it as a percentage is just a fraction with denominator fixed at 100; "of" means multiply. § Percentages

$$|a| = \begin{cases} a & a \ge 0\\ -a & a < 0\end{cases}, \qquad |a+b| \le |a|+|b|$$

Read it as absolute value is distance from zero, and a detour (triangle inequality) never beats the direct route. § Absolute value

02Algebra

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

Read it as the two points where a parabola crosses zero, found by completing the square once and for all. § Quadratic formula

$$(x+y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k} y^{k}$$

Read it as expanding a power counts, for each term, how many ways you can pick $y$ from $k$ of the $n$ factors. § Binomial theorem

$$a^m a^n = a^{m+n}, \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$

Read it as multiplying powers stacks the repetitions, so exponents add; powering a power multiplies them. § Laws of exponents

$$\log(ab) = \log a + \log b, \qquad \log(a^k) = k \log a$$

Read it as logarithms turn multiplication into addition and powers into products — the reason they tame likelihoods. § Logarithm laws

$$a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$$

Read it as a difference of squares always factors; the cross terms cancel. § Difference of squares

03Sets, Logic and Proof

$$|A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B|$$

Read it as add the two sets, then subtract the overlap you double-counted. § Inclusion–exclusion

$$\neg(P \land Q) \equiv \neg P \lor \neg Q$$

Read it as De Morgan: to deny "both", it is enough that at least one fails. § Logical equivalence

$$\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}$$

Read it as count ordered selections, then divide out the $k!$ orderings you don't care about. § Combinations

$$\big[P(0) \;\land\; \forall n\, (P(n) \Rightarrow P(n{+}1))\big] \;\Rightarrow\; \forall n\, P(n)$$

Read it as knock over the first domino and show each domino topples the next — then they all fall. § Induction

04Functions

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

Read it as slope is rise over run — how much output you buy per unit of input. § Slope

$$(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$$

Read it as feed the output of the inner machine into the outer one; deep networks are long chains of exactly this. § Composition

$$f^{-1}(f(x)) = x, \qquad f(f^{-1}(y)) = y$$

Read it as an inverse function exactly undoes what the function did, in either order. § Inverse functions

$$a_n = a_1 r^{\,n-1}, \qquad \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a r^k = a\,\frac{1-r^n}{1-r}$$

Read it as multiply by the same ratio each step; the sum telescopes into one closed form. § Geometric sequences

05Geometry

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

Read it as in a right triangle, the squares on the legs tile the square on the hypotenuse. § Pythagorean theorem

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}$$

Read it as distance is Pythagoras applied to the coordinate differences. § Distance formula

$$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = \sum_i a_i b_i = \|\mathbf{a}\|\,\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta$$

Read it as the dot product measures alignment: positive when vectors agree, zero when perpendicular. § Dot product

$$\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{a} = \frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{b}}\,\mathbf{b}$$

Read it as the shadow of $\mathbf{a}$ along $\mathbf{b}$: keep only the component that points $\mathbf{b}$'s way. § Projection

06Trigonometry

$$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$$

Read it as Pythagoras on the unit circle: every angle's coordinates lie one unit from the origin. § Pythagorean identities

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$$

Read it as Pythagoras with a correction term for the angle not being right. § Law of cosines

$$\sin(\alpha+\beta) = \sin\alpha\cos\beta + \cos\alpha\sin\beta$$

Read it as rotating by two angles in a row mixes their sines and cosines — it's matrix rotation, componentwise. § Angle addition

$$e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$$

Read it as the exponential of an imaginary number is a point on the unit circle; growth becomes rotation. § Euler's formula

07Calculus

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$$

Read it as the slope of the secant line as the two points merge — instantaneous rate of change. § Derivative

$$\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))\,g'(x)$$

Read it as sensitivities multiply through a pipeline — the seed of backpropagation. § Chain rule

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a), \quad F' = f$$

Read it as total accumulation equals the change in any antiderivative — integration undoes differentiation. § Fundamental theorem

$$\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$$

Read it as the product rule run backwards: trade one integral for a hopefully easier one. § Integration by parts

$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^n$$

Read it as rebuild a smooth function from its derivatives at one point — a polynomial that matches ever more closely. § Taylor series

$$\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x\to a}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} \quad \text{(for } \tfrac{0}{0}, \tfrac{\infty}{\infty}\text{)}$$

Read it as when both parts vanish or blow up, compare their speeds instead of their values. § L'Hôpital

08Linear Algebra

$$A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}$$

Read it as along an eigenvector, the matrix acts as pure stretching by $\lambda$ — no rotation, no mixing. § Eigenvalues

$$A = P D P^{-1}$$

Read it as change into the eigenbasis, where the matrix is just a diagonal list of stretches. § Diagonalization

$$A = U \Sigma V^{\!\top}$$

Read it as every matrix is a rotation, then axis-aligned stretching, then another rotation. § SVD

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) + \dim\ker(A) = n$$

Read it as dimensions are conserved: what the map doesn't kill, it keeps. § Rank

$$\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_i x_i^2}, \qquad \|\mathbf{x}\|_1 = \textstyle\sum_i |x_i|$$

Read it as two ways to measure a vector's size: straight-line length versus total coordinate travel. § Norms

09Multivariable and Matrix Calculus

$$\nabla f = \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}, \dots, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}\right)$$

Read it as one slope per input direction, stacked into the vector that points steepest uphill. § Gradient

$$\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}$$

Read it as influence flows along every path and the paths add up. § Multivariable chain rule

$$J_{ij} = \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}, \qquad H_{ij} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_i\,\partial x_j}$$

Read it as the Jacobian is the local linear map; the Hessian is the local curvature of a scalar landscape. § Jacobian · § Hessian

$$\nabla f(x^*) = \lambda\, \nabla g(x^*)$$

Read it as at a constrained optimum, the objective can only improve by leaving the constraint surface — the gradients must be parallel. § Lagrange multipliers

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{x}} = \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{y}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\right)^{\!\top} \frac{\partial L}{\partial \mathbf{y}}$$

Read it as backpropagation: pull the loss gradient backwards through each layer by multiplying with the transposed Jacobian. § Backprop as chain rule

10Differential Equations

$$\frac{dy}{dt} = ky \;\Rightarrow\; y(t) = y_0 e^{kt}$$

Read it as when growth is proportional to size, size is exponential in time. § Exponential growth

$$\frac{dy}{dt} = ry\left(1 - \frac{y}{K}\right)$$

Read it as exponential growth with a brake that tightens as $y$ nears the capacity $K$ — the S-curve. § Logistic equation

$$y'' + by' + cy = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; r^2 + br + c = 0$$

Read it as guess $e^{rt}$ and the ODE becomes algebra; the roots' sign and realness decide decay, growth, or oscillation. § Characteristic equation

$$\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A\mathbf{x} \;\Rightarrow\; \mathbf{x}(t) = e^{At}\mathbf{x}_0$$

Read it as a linear system's whole future is one matrix exponential applied to where it starts. § Matrix exponential

$$y_{n+1} = y_n + h\, f(t_n, y_n)$$

Read it as Euler's method: follow the current slope for a small step, then re-aim. § Euler's method

11Probability

$$P(H \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid H)\,P(H)}{P(E)}$$

Read it as belief after evidence is proportional to how well the hypothesis predicted the evidence, times prior belief. § Bayes' theorem

$$\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_x x\,P(x), \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\big[(X-\mathbb{E}X)^2\big]$$

Read it as the probability-weighted average, and the average squared distance from it — center and spread. § Expectation · § Variance

$$p(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

Read it as the bell curve: log-density falls off as squared distance from the mean, in units of $\sigma$. § Gaussian

$$\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mu_X)(Y-\mu_Y)]$$

Read it as do the two variables tend to be above (or below) their means together? § Covariance

$$\frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \;\xrightarrow{d}\; \mathcal{N}(0,1)$$

Read it as averages of many independent draws become Gaussian, whatever the source distribution, with spread shrinking like $1/\sqrt{n}$. § Central Limit Theorem

12Stochastic Processes

$$\pi = \pi P$$

Read it as the stationary distribution is unchanged by one more step of the chain — the fixed point the chain flows toward. § Stationary distributions

$$df(X_t) = f'(X_t)\,dX_t + \tfrac{1}{2} f''(X_t)\,\sigma_t^2\,dt$$

Read it as the chain rule for noisy paths: randomness is so jagged that curvature contributes a first-order term. § Itô's lemma

$$dX_t = \mu(X_t)\,dt + \sigma(X_t)\,dW_t$$

Read it as each instant, move with a deterministic drift plus a Gaussian kick scaled by $\sigma$. § SDEs

$$dX_t = \nabla \log p(X_t)\,dt + \sqrt{2}\,dW_t$$

Read it as Langevin dynamics: drift uphill on log-probability with noise, and you end up sampling from $p$. § Langevin dynamics

13Statistics

$$\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_\theta \sum_i \log p(x_i \mid \theta)$$

Read it as pick the parameters under which the observed data would have been least surprising. § Maximum likelihood

$$p(\theta \mid x) \propto p(x \mid \theta)\, p(\theta)$$

Read it as the posterior blends what the data say (likelihood) with what you believed before (prior). § Posterior

$$\hat\beta = (X^{\!\top}X)^{-1} X^{\!\top} \mathbf{y}$$

Read it as the least-squares coefficients project the targets onto the span of the features. § Least squares

$$\bar{x} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\, \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$

Read it as a confidence interval: the estimate, give or take a few standard errors. § Confidence intervals

$$\log p(x) = \mathrm{ELBO}(q) + \mathrm{KL}\big(q(z)\,\|\,p(z \mid x)\big)$$

Read it as the evidence splits into a computable lower bound plus the gap to the true posterior — raise one, shrink the other. § ELBO

$$\alpha = \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{p(x')\,q(x \mid x')}{p(x)\,q(x' \mid x)}\right)$$

Read it as Metropolis–Hastings: accept a proposed move with probability tilted toward higher-probability states, and the chain samples $p$. § Metropolis–Hastings

14Fourier Analysis and Signals

$$\hat{f}(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\, e^{-i\omega t}\, dt$$

Read it as correlate the signal against every pure frequency; $\hat f(\omega)$ says how much of that wave is inside. § Fourier transform

$$(f * g)(t) = \int f(\tau)\, g(t - \tau)\, d\tau$$

Read it as slide one function across the other and accumulate the overlap — echoes, blurs, filters. § Convolution

$$\widehat{f * g} = \hat{f}\,\cdot\,\hat{g}$$

Read it as convolution in time is plain multiplication in frequency — the trick that makes filtering (and the FFT) fast. § Convolution theorem

$$f_s > 2B$$

Read it as to capture a signal faithfully, sample at more than twice its highest frequency, or aliasing folds the spectrum. § Sampling theorem

15Optimization

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta\, \nabla L(\theta_t)$$

Read it as step downhill, with step size $\eta$; repeat until the gradient vanishes. § Gradient descent

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta\, \nabla \hat{L}_{B}(\theta_t)$$

Read it as the same step, but with the gradient estimated from a random mini-batch — noisy, cheap, and unbiased. § SGD

$$v_{t+1} = \beta v_t + \nabla L(\theta_t), \qquad \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta\, v_{t+1}$$

Read it as momentum: a heavy ball accumulates velocity, smoothing noise and powering through shallow valleys. § Momentum

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta\, \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon}$$

Read it as Adam: divide the averaged gradient by its typical size, so every parameter gets a step scaled to its own history. § Adam

$$f\big(\lambda x + (1{-}\lambda) y\big) \le \lambda f(x) + (1{-}\lambda) f(y)$$

Read it as convexity: the chord lies above the curve, so every local minimum is global. § Convex functions

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - H^{-1} \nabla L(\theta_t)$$

Read it as Newton's method: rescale the step by the curvature, jumping straight to the quadratic model's minimum. § Newton's method

16Information Theory

$$H(p) = -\sum_x p(x) \log p(x)$$

Read it as average surprise: the minimum bits per symbol needed to encode draws from $p$. § Entropy

$$H(p, q) = -\sum_x p(x) \log q(x)$$

Read it as the bits you actually pay when you code with model $q$ but the world runs on $p$ — every ML training loss's ancestor. § Cross-entropy

$$\mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,q) = \sum_x p(x) \log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)} \;\ge\; 0$$

Read it as the extra bits wasted by believing $q$ when the truth is $p$; zero only when they match. § KL divergence

$$I(X;Y) = H(X) - H(X \mid Y)$$

Read it as mutual information: how many bits of uncertainty about $X$ disappear once you learn $Y$. § Mutual information

$$\hat{H} = \arg\min_H \big[ L(H) + L(D \mid H) \big]$$

Read it as the best theory minimizes bits-to-state-the-theory plus bits-to-state-the-data-given-it — Occam with units. § MDL

17Discrete Mathematics and Computation

$$T(n) = a\,T(n/b) + f(n)$$

Read it as the master recurrence: split into $a$ pieces of size $n/b$, pay $f(n)$ to combine; the theorem reads off the total. § Master theorem

$$\sum_{v \in V} \deg(v) = 2\,|E|$$

Read it as every edge donates two endpoint-slots, so total degree is twice the edge count. § Graph theory

$$f(n) = O(g(n)) \iff \exists\, c, n_0:\; f(n) \le c\,g(n) \;\; \forall n \ge n_0$$

Read it as eventually, $f$ is bounded by a constant multiple of $g$ — growth rate, ignoring constants. § Asymptotic notation

$$G(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} a_n x^n$$

Read it as pack a whole sequence into one function; multiplying generating functions convolves the sequences. § Generating functions

18Numerical Mathematics

$$\mathrm{fl}(x) = x(1+\delta), \qquad |\delta| \le \epsilon_{\text{mach}}$$

Read it as every stored number is the true one times a tiny relative error, bounded by machine epsilon. § Floating-point error

$$\kappa(A) = \|A\|\,\|A^{-1}\|$$

Read it as the condition number: how much a problem amplifies input error into output error, algorithm aside. § Conditioning

$$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$$

Read it as follow the tangent line to where it hits zero; near a root, correct digits double each step. § Newton's method

$$\log \sum_i e^{x_i} = m + \log \sum_i e^{x_i - m}, \quad m = \max_i x_i$$

Read it as subtract the max before exponentiating so nothing overflows — the trick inside every stable softmax. § Log-sum-exp

19Machine Learning

$$P(y{=}1 \mid x) = \sigma(w^{\!\top} x + b), \qquad \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}$$

Read it as logistic regression: a linear score squashed to a probability. § Logistic regression

$$L = -\sum_i \big[ y_i \log \hat{p}_i + (1-y_i)\log(1-\hat{p}_i) \big]$$

Read it as log loss: pay heavily for confident wrong answers — cross-entropy between labels and predictions. § Log loss

$$\mathbb{E}\big[(y - \hat{f})^2\big] = \text{Bias}^2 + \text{Variance} + \sigma^2$$

Read it as error splits into being systematically off, being unstable across datasets, and irreducible noise. § Bias–variance

$$K(x, x') = \langle \phi(x), \phi(x') \rangle$$

Read it as the kernel trick: compute inner products in a huge feature space without ever visiting it. § Kernel trick

$$\min_{\{c_k\}} \sum_i \min_k \|x_i - c_k\|^2$$

Read it as k-means: place $k$ centers so every point sits close to its nearest one. § K-means

20Statistical Learning Theory

$$n \;\ge\; \frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\ln|\mathcal{H}| + \ln\frac{1}{\delta}\right)$$

Read it as enough samples to rule out every bad hypothesis in the class, with confidence $1-\delta$. § Finite class bounds

$$R(h) \;\le\; \hat{R}(h) + O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{d \ln n + \ln(1/\delta)}{n}}\right)$$

Read it as true error is at most training error plus a complexity tax growing with VC dimension $d$ and shrinking with data $n$. § Generalization bounds

$$\hat{\mathfrak{R}}_n(\mathcal{F}) = \mathbb{E}_\sigma\!\left[\sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \sigma_i f(x_i)\right]$$

Read it as Rademacher complexity: how well the class can fit pure coin-flip noise — the sharper measure of capacity. § Rademacher complexity

21Deep Learning

$$\mathbf{a} = \sigma(W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})$$

Read it as a layer: mix the inputs linearly, then bend the result — stack these and you can approximate anything. § Neural networks

$$\operatorname{softmax}(z)_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}$$

Read it as exponentiate scores and normalize: bigger scores get exponentially more of the probability. § Softmax

$$\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\!\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

Read it as each query scores every key, the scores become weights, and each token's output is a weighted blend of values. § Self-attention

$$\mathrm{LN}(x) = \gamma \odot \frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}} + \beta$$

Read it as re-center and re-scale each token's activations so training signals stay in a healthy range. § Layer norm

$$\mathbf{h}_{l+1} = \mathbf{h}_l + F(\mathbf{h}_l)$$

Read it as a residual connection: each block adds a correction to an identity highway, so gradients flow undamped. § Residual connections

$$L(N) \approx \left(\frac{N_c}{N}\right)^{\alpha}$$

Read it as loss falls as a power law in model size — smooth, predictable improvement over orders of magnitude. § Scaling laws

22Generative Models

$$\mathrm{ELBO} = \mathbb{E}_{q(z|x)}\big[\log p(x \mid z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q(z \mid x)\,\|\,p(z)\big)$$

Read it as reconstruct well, but keep the latent code close to the prior — the VAE's two-term bargain. § ELBO

$$z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$

Read it as move the randomness outside the network so gradients can flow through the sampling step. § Reparameterization

$$\min_G \max_D \; \mathbb{E}_{x}\big[\log D(x)\big] + \mathbb{E}_{z}\big[\log(1 - D(G(z)))\big]$$

Read it as a forger and a detective train against each other until fakes are indistinguishable. § GANs

$$q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0,\; (1-\bar\alpha_t)\, I\big)$$

Read it as after $t$ noising steps the image is a faded copy of the original plus Gaussian noise — and you can jump there in one line. § DDPM

$$s(x) = \nabla_x \log p(x)$$

Read it as the score: at every point, which way is "more probable" — the vector field diffusion models learn to follow backwards. § Score functions

23Large Language Models

$$L = -\sum_t \log p_\theta\big(x_t \mid x_{<t}\big)$$

Read it as pretraining is next-token cross-entropy: compress the corpus one prediction at a time. § Pretraining objective

$$L(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^{\alpha}} + \frac{B}{D^{\beta}}$$

Read it as loss decomposes into an irreducible floor plus penalties for too few parameters and too little data — balance them (Chinchilla). § Compute-optimal scaling

$$p_i \propto \exp(z_i / T)$$

Read it as temperature reshapes the sampling distribution: $T \to 0$ is greedy, large $T$ flattens toward uniform. § Temperature

$$\max_\pi\; \mathbb{E}\big[r(x, y)\big] - \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}\big)$$

Read it as RLHF: chase the reward, but stay within a KL leash of the pretrained model. § RLHF

$$L_{\text{DPO}} = -\log \sigma\!\left(\beta \log\frac{\pi(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log\frac{\pi(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)}\right)$$

Read it as DPO: push preferred completions up and rejected ones down, directly, with no explicit reward model or RL loop. § DPO

$$W' = W + BA, \qquad B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r},\; A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k},\; r \ll d$$

Read it as LoRA: fine-tune only a low-rank correction to each frozen weight matrix. § LoRA

24Decision and Game Theory

$$\mathbb{E}[U(a)] = \sum_s P(s)\, U(a, s)$$

Read it as score each action by its probability-weighted utility over possible worlds; pick the best. § Expected utility

$$a^* = \arg\max_a \; \mathbb{E}_{\theta \sim p(\theta \mid \text{data})}\big[U(a, \theta)\big]$$

Read it as the Bayes-optimal act maximizes expected utility under your posterior beliefs. § Bayesian decisions

$$u_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}^*) \;\ge\; u_i(s_i, s_{-i}^*) \quad \forall\, s_i$$

Read it as Nash equilibrium: no player can gain by deviating alone — everyone's strategy is a best response to everyone else's. § Nash equilibrium

$$\max_x \min_y \; u(x, y) = \min_y \max_x \; u(x, y)$$

Read it as in a zero-sum game with mixed strategies, playing for the worst case is optimal — the minimax value exists. § Minimax

25Reinforcement Learning

$$G_t = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \gamma^k\, r_{t+k+1}$$

Read it as the return: all future reward, with tomorrow discounted by $\gamma$ per step of delay. § Returns

$$V^\pi(s) = \mathbb{E}_\pi\big[r + \gamma\, V^\pi(s')\big]$$

Read it as a state's value is the immediate reward plus the discounted value of wherever you land — recursion over time. § Bellman expectation

$$V^*(s) = \max_a \; \mathbb{E}\big[r + \gamma\, V^*(s')\big]$$

Read it as the optimal value assumes you take the best action now and act optimally forever after. § Bellman optimality

$$Q(s,a) \mathrel{+}= \alpha\big[\, r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s', a') - Q(s,a) \,\big]$$

Read it as Q-learning: nudge your estimate toward the sampled one-step Bellman target. § Q-learning

$$\delta_t = r_{t+1} + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$$

Read it as the TD error: how much better or worse things went than your value function expected. § TD learning

$$\nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}_\pi\big[\, G_t\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t) \,\big]$$

Read it as the policy gradient: make actions that led to high return more probable, in proportion to that return. § Policy gradients

26Planning and Search

$$f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$$

Read it as A* ranks a node by cost paid so far plus estimated cost to go; with an admissible $h$, the first goal found is optimal. § A*

$$a^* = \arg\max_a \left[ \bar{Q}(s,a) + c \sqrt{\frac{\ln N(s)}{N(s,a)}} \right]$$

Read it as UCT: pick the child with the best average value plus an exploration bonus for being under-visited. § UCT

$$h(n) \le h^*(n)$$

Read it as an admissible heuristic never overestimates the true remaining cost — optimism that keeps A* honest. § Admissible heuristics

$$\text{minimax}(n) = \begin{cases}\max_{c} \text{minimax}(c) & \text{our move}\\ \min_{c} \text{minimax}(c) & \text{their move}\end{cases}$$

Read it as assume the opponent plays their best against your best, all the way down the game tree. § Minimax

27Causality

$$P(y \mid do(x)) \;\ne\; P(y \mid x)$$

Read it as forcing $x$ is not the same as observing $x$ — intervention cuts the arrows into $x$, conditioning does not. § Interventions

$$P(y \mid do(x)) = \sum_z P(y \mid x, z)\, P(z)$$

Read it as the backdoor adjustment: average over the confounders $z$, and observational data answers a causal question. § Backdoor criterion

$$\mathrm{ATE} = \mathbb{E}\big[Y(1) - Y(0)\big]$$

Read it as the average treatment effect: the mean gap between each unit's two potential outcomes — treated versus not. § Potential outcomes

$$X_i := f_i(\mathrm{pa}(X_i),\, U_i)$$

Read it as a structural equation: each variable is a mechanism applied to its parents plus its own noise — the ":=" flows one way. § Structural causal models

28Dynamical Systems and Control

$$\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A\mathbf{x} \;\text{ stable} \iff \operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) < 0 \;\; \forall i$$

Read it as a linear system settles to rest exactly when every eigenvalue's real part decays. § Stability

$$V(x) > 0,\;\; \dot{V}(x) \le 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{stable}$$

Read it as a Lyapunov function is an energy that never increases along trajectories — if one exists, the system can't escape. § Lyapunov functions

$$\hat{x} \leftarrow \hat{x}^- + K\,(y - H\hat{x}^-)$$

Read it as the Kalman update: correct your prediction by the surprise in the measurement, weighted by the gain $K$ (trust in data vs. model). § Kalman filter

$$J = \int_0^\infty \big( x^{\!\top} Q\, x + u^{\!\top} R\, u \big)\, dt$$
